## Their plan, written before the visit

### Plan — P02 (Marcus)

#### What I usually do on a new site
- I go straight for the actual tool/calculator, not the marketing copy. If there's a hero section
  selling me on how great it is before I can touch anything, that's already a small strike.
- I check whether it tells me *how* it calculates things — assumptions, sim methodology, what patch/
  phase the data is for. If a number shows up with no explanation of where it came from, I don't
  trust it. Research backs this up: trust for calculator-style tools comes from visible assumptions,
  defensible inputs, and being able to understand the result after the fact, not just a slick UI.
- I look for signs of who built it and whether it's maintained — last update date, changelog, GitHub
  link, Discord. Dead or abandoned sim tools quietly drift out of date with patches and start lying
  to you.
- I'm going to compare every number I see against what WoWSims and Seventy Upgrades would tell me
  for the same gear/talents. If it disagrees without explaining why, I assume it's wrong until proven
  otherwise, not the other way around.
- I decide fast. Studies say people size up a site's trustworthiness in the first few seconds off
  layout and clarity alone — I'm not that extreme, but if I can't find my class/spec and get to real
  numbers in a couple clicks, I'm out.
- I don't fill out forms or make an account just to look around. If it gate-keeps the actual sim
  behind a login, that's a big turn-off.

#### What I'm hoping to get out of this visit
Figure out if this thing can replace or supplement WoWSims + Seventy Upgrades for my Fury Warrior —
can it sim my rotation and gear choices with numbers I can actually trust, or is it just another
half-baked BiS list with a UI on top. I want to see stat weights, a way to input my actual gear, and
ideally a rotation/sim output, then decide if it's worth bookmarking.

## Their report, written after the visit

### P02: Marcus

#### In one paragraph
Project Defeat is a TBC Classic Phase 2 toolset — character planner, BiS/ranked gear, talents, raid buffs, a prototype attack-table simulator, and stat weights — built around a Wowhead-sourced tier list and its own per-slot gear rankings. For a Fury Warrior main who lives in WoWSims and Seventy Upgrades, the planner half (gear, BiS, talents, compare, buffs) is legitimately solid and internally consistent. The simulation half is honest about being incomplete — it says outright that it doesn't model Whirlwind, Heroic Strike, or rage for Fury — which means it can't replace WoWSims for real optimization yet, but it doesn't try to hide that gap either.

#### First impression
Landing page (step 1) reads as a TBC Classic companion site — moody Warcraft splash art, "Project Defeat," five feature cards (Character Planner, Raid Composition, Spec Tier Lists, Raids, Professions). No login wall, no ad clutter. I assumed it was aimed at raiders planning gear/comp, which held up. No sim was mentioned on the front page at all — I only found it as a tab once inside the planner (step 17), which undersold the thing I actually came for.

#### What I came for, and what I actually did
I came to see if this could sim or at least gear-plan my Orc Fury Warrior as well as WoWSims/Seventy Upgrades do. I built the character (Horde/Orc/Warrior/Fury, steps 2-9), equipped head/neck/shoulders off the ranked BiS list and watched stats update live (steps 11-14), checked the talent calculator (step 15) and build save/share model (step 16), ran the Simulation tab twice — once bare, once with Battle Shout toggled on (steps 18-19, 27-31) — read the Stat Priority/EP weight table (step 20), ran a Gear Comparison between two head items (steps 22-24), and checked the Spec Tier List for source honesty (step 25).

#### Where I got stuck or confused
- Step 22-23: Gear Comparison defaulted to comparing my equipped melee helm (Destroyer Battle-Helm) against "Crown of the Sun," a healing/spell-power item. For a Fury Warrior that's a nonsense pairing — I expected it to default to the #1 vs #2 ranked item for my spec, not a random cross-role item. I had to manually `select` a sane alternative (Furious Gizmatic Goggles, step 24) to get a comparison that meant anything.
- Step 12: I equipped gear but never put a weapon on before running the sim, and nothing warned me "no weapon equipped" — it just silently reported Weapon Damage: 0 and a laughably low 64.6 DPS. A real tool should flag that instead of quietly returning a number that looks like an error.

#### What I liked
- Stats updated live and added up exactly right every time I checked by hand (Battle Shout showed +306 AP and my total went 814 to 1120 exactly, step 28).
- The BiS list cites item IDs, exact boss/instance, and crafting mats (step 11) — that's more provenance than most fan BiS lists bother with.
- The Simulation tab's self-disclosure is the best thing on the site: it names exactly what it left out for Fury (Whirlwind, Heroic Strike/rage) and why, instead of hiding it (step 18-19).
- The Spec Tier List openly credits Wowhead as the source with a link, and explicitly says it is not what the gear rankings are built from (step 25) — no pretending someone else's conclusion is their own theorycrafting.
- Stat weights passed my one instant sanity check: Strength came out to 2.00 normalized against Attack Power = 1.00, which is correct for a Warrior (step 17).
- No forced account, no login wall to use any of this.

#### What I never found, or gave up on
I didn't check Raid Composition, Raids, or Professions — not because anything went wrong, but because none of that answers "can this replace my sim," which is the only question Marcus actually has. I also never got the DPS number into a range I'd call real (topped out at 89, step 31), since I skipped weapons and only equipped three slots — that's on my test setup, not a site bug, but it means I can't say whether the fully-modeled portion (Bloodthirst + white damage) is numerically accurate, only that it's internally consistent.

#### Words or labels I didn't understand
"Role-aware prototype" (step 17) — took a second read to realize it means the sim treats specs differently rather than something about tank/healer roles. Otherwise labeling was clear and used real WoW terms throughout.

#### Three things I would change
1. Default Gear Comparison to the two top-ranked items in that slot for the character's actual spec, not an arbitrary pairing across roles.
2. Warn when running a simulation with empty weapon slots (or any equipped-gear gap that guts the number) instead of silently returning a near-zero DPS.
3. Put a one-line mention of the Simulation tool on the homepage card copy, or at least name which specs get full rotation coverage before someone spends time gearing a character it can't fully model.

#### Would I come back? Would I recommend it, and to whom?
For BiS lists, talent planning, and buff/stat-weight sanity checks — yes, I'd bookmark it, and I'd point a newer raider at it over piecing together three separate Wowhead tabs. For actual rotation DPS optimization on my Fury Warrior specifically, no, not yet — I'm not dropping WoWSims until Fury gets real Whirlwind/Heroic Strike/rage modeling. I'd tell my Discord it's worth knowing about and worth revisiting once the sim matures, not worth switching to today.

#### Why I stopped
I'd answered my own question — planner's good, sim's honestly not there yet for Fury — and confirmed it with a buff-toggle check that the math is at least self-consistent. Nothing left to test was going to change that verdict, so I ended it there (step 32) rather than pad the session.

#### Plan versus reality
Plan.md said I'd check for visible methodology/assumptions, provenance on data, signs of active maintenance, and would cross-check numbers against WoWSims/Seventy Upgrades before trusting them. I did all of that except the direct side-by-side number cross-check (no WoWSims tab open to compare against, per the study's browser restriction) — instead I did the next best thing, hand-verifying the tool's own internal arithmetic (buff math, EP normalization), which is exactly the kind of check my plan described. I didn't get to test whether it's actively maintained (no changelog/version found), which I'd said I'd look for.
