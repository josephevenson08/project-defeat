### P07: Ines, 34, screen reader user

Blind since birth, and uses the NVDA screen reader with Firefox every day. Doesn't play herself, but
her partner does, and she is the "logistics" person for his guild's raids. She organises the roster on
Discord, which works well with a screen reader, so she knows the classes, roles and raid names well.
Expert screen reader user. **Efficient: navigates by headings and landmarks first, and loses patience
with unlabelled buttons and anything that only makes sense visually.** Her partner sent her the link.
Wants to see whether it could help plan raid groups. Desktop 1440×900.

**Strict constraint:** you cannot see the screen. **Never open a screenshot and never use `look`.**
Perceive the page only through `outline`, `aria`, and what `press` reports has focus. Act only with
`press` and `type`. Use Tab and Shift+Tab to move between controls, Enter and Space to activate, and
arrow keys inside widgets.

## Their plan, written before the visit

### Plan — P07: Ines

#### What I usually do in my first minutes on a new site

- I hit the heading list first (NVDA's "1" or the elements list). If I land on an H1 straight away and
  there's a sensible run of H2s under it, I relax a little — someone thought about this.
- Then I sweep the landmarks/regions (D key territory): banner, nav, main, footer. That tells me the
  shape of the page before I read a word of content, the same way a sighted person's eye does a quick
  pass over the layout.
- I do not read top to bottom like a document. I skip straight to main and skim from there, only going
  back to nav or footer if I need them.
- Unlabelled buttons and generic links ("click here", "link") are where I lose patience fastest —
  if I hit two or three of those in the first minute, I start expecting the rest of the site to be the
  same and I brace for it.
- I try one real task early (not just poking around) because that's the only way to know if a site
  actually works, not just whether it announces itself politely.
- If a form or a widget swallows my focus, or Tab throws me somewhere I didn't expect, that's usually
  where I decide whether I'm staying or bailing.

#### What I'm hoping to get out of this visit

My partner raids and I do the roster logistics for his guild on Discord, which works fine with NVDA.
He sent me this link saying it might help with planning raid groups. I want to find out, using only
the keyboard and what NVDA would tell me, whether I could actually use it for that — build or read a
raid roster, see who's in what role — or whether it's another site that only makes sense if you can
see it.

## Their report, written after the visit

### P07: Ines

#### In one paragraph
This is a Burning Crusade Classic raid-planning toolkit — character planner, raid composition/roster builder, spec tier lists, raid loot, professions — and "Raid Composition" is exactly the roster feature my partner sent me here for. Once inside it, the screen was genuinely well built for a screen reader: real landmarks, real heading levels, buttons that say exactly what they do. Then I typed into the Raid date field on the very form I needed, and the keyboard simply stopped working — no way forward, no way back, no explanation. That's where my visit ended.

#### First impression
After the first screen (step 1, step 2) I understood what this was and who it's for: a TBC Classic raid-planning site, aimed at players and the people organizing them, which lines up with what my partner told me. But forming that impression took work — the front page is just one heading ("Project Defeat") and a nav landmark called "Choose a section" holding five buttons. Each button's whole accessible name is an eyebrow tag, a title and a full sentence of description all run together with no punctuse break, e.g. "BUFF COVERAGE Raid Composition Plan a 10 or 25-player roster and see which raid buffs and target debuffs it actually brings — and what one more seat would buy you" (step 2, step 4). So yes, I was right about what it is and who it's for, but I had to sit through five paragraph-length button names to work that out, when five short headings would have told me in seconds.

#### What I came for, and what I actually did
I came to see whether I could build or read a raid roster for my partner's guild. I tabbed to the "Raid Composition" button and activated it (step 4, step 5), landed on a screen with a proper heading and landmark structure (step 6), and read the instructions, which specifically promised "press Move on their seat and then the seat you want them in — or drag them there" (step 7) — exactly the kind of non-drag alternative I was hoping to find. I started filling the raid-details form in the order it was presented — title, then date — planning to work my way down to "Add a spec" and put a character in. I never got there. I got stuck on the Raid date field (steps 20–29) and that ended the visit.

#### Where I got stuck or confused
- **Step 5**: I pressed Enter on "Raid Composition" and the page changed underneath me, but focus landed on "nothing (page body)." I only knew anything had happened because I separately checked the heading list (step 6). On a real page with NVDA, that keypress would have produced total silence — I'd have assumed nothing happened and pressed Enter again.
- **Steps 20–29, the big one**: Tab moved me onto the "Raid date" field. I pressed Tab again — still on Raid date (step 21). Again — still there (step 22). I tried Shift+Tab to back out — still there (step 23). I checked whether some dialog or calendar popup had opened that NVDA just wasn't telling me about — nothing in the tree (step 24). I tried Escape — no change (step 25). I typed an actual date into the field in case it was waiting on a value — no change (step 26), and Tab still wouldn't leave it afterward (step 27). I even tried Firefox's own back shortcut — still parked on Raid date (step 29). Four distinct escape attempts, all failed, no announced reason. That is a complete keyboard trap on the fourth field of the one form I needed.

#### What I liked
- Once inside "Raid Composition," the landmark and heading structure was some of the best I've heard on a first visit — main, header, nav, and clearly named sections ("Raid details," "Role balance," "Add a spec," "Groups," "Raid-wide," "Party buffs," "Debuffs") at proper h2/h3/h4 levels (step 6, step 9).
- The class/spec picker buttons are fully self-describing — "Add Restoration Shaman," "Add Balance Druid" — never just a bare "Add" I'd have to guess about (step 9).
- The raid-size control is a real toggle button pair with correct pressed state ("10-player [pressed=false]", "25-player [pressed=true]"), and the current section in the nav is marked `[current=page]` (steps 12, 16–17). Small things, but they're exactly the details that tell me someone building this knows what they're doing.
- The on-page copy volunteers a non-drag way to reposition a player before I even asked (step 7) — most sites with drag-and-drop don't think about this at all.

#### What I never found, or gave up on
I never added a single player to a roster, never got to try the "Move" mechanic I was hopeful about, never saw buff/debuff coverage change, and never visited Character Planner, Spec Tier Lists, Raids, or Professions beyond hearing their names in the nav. The Raid date trap ended things before any of that.

#### Words or labels I didn't understand
Nothing was worded unclearly — labels were plain and specific throughout. The one thing that made me pause: the Druid options were "Balance," "Feral (Bear)," "Feral (Cat)," "Restoration," and "Dreamstate" (step 9). I do this roster logistics on Discord regularly and I've never heard "Dreamstate" as a spec name — not a comprehension problem, just unfamiliar.

#### Three things I would change
1. Fix whatever is trapping keyboard focus on the Raid date field (step 20 onward) — until that's fixed, no keyboard-only or screen-reader user can get past the second field of this form, full stop.
2. Move focus to the new screen's heading when a nav button changes the view (step 5), so a screen reader actually announces that navigation happened.
3. Give the front-page section buttons real, separate headings instead of packing an eyebrow tag, title, and full sentence into one button name (step 2) — and consider a skip link, since every return trip to the top means re-hearing the same six section buttons before reaching content.

#### Would I come back? Would I recommend it, and to whom?
Not as it stands — I couldn't finish the one task I came for. I'd tell my partner the idea is exactly right, and that someone already thought about non-drag seating, but that I hit a hard wall on the very first form and would need it fixed before I could actually use this for our raids. I wouldn't recommend it to anyone else who uses a keyboard or screen reader yet — but I'd be willing to try again if I heard the date field got fixed.

#### Why I stopped
A complete, reproducible keyboard trap on the Raid date field (steps 20–29): Tab, Shift+Tab, Escape, typing a value, and a browser-back shortcut all failed to move focus away, with nothing in the accessibility tree to explain why. That's not a "figure it out" problem, it's a locked door.

#### Plan versus reality
Plan.md said I'd sweep landmarks and headings first, try one real task early, and that an unlabelled button or getting stuck in a form would decide whether I stay or go. That's almost exactly what happened: the sweep (steps 1–2) showed a front page thin on real structure; I got into the real task fast (step 5); and a stuck form field (steps 20–29) was precisely the kind of moment I predicted would end the visit — and it did.
