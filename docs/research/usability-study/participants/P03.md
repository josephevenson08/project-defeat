## Their plan, written before the visit

### Plan — P03 (Priya)

#### What I personally do in the first minutes on a new site
- I glance at it for maybe 3-5 seconds before I decide if it's legit and worth my time — first impressions are basically instant, so if it looks broken or cluttered on my phone I'm out.
- I don't read, I scan — I'm looking for a big obvious heading and a button that matches whatever I clicked the link for (in this case, raid groups).
- I try to do the one thing I came for immediately. I don't explore menus or read help text unless I get stuck.
- Because I'm one-handed, usually holding my coffee or half-listening in a meeting, I expect buttons to be big enough to hit without zooming in, and important stuff near the bottom half of the screen where my thumb already is.
- If something takes more than a couple of taps to figure out, or I mis-tap because a button's too small, I'm annoyed and I'll bail and just go back to my spreadsheet.
- I don't create accounts or fill out long forms on a whim from a DM link — that's a "later, at my laptop" thing unless it's obviously fast.

#### What I'm hoping to get out of this visit
Sketch out group assignments for Tuesday's Serpentshrine Cavern raid — basically see if I can drag/drop or otherwise arrange 25 players into groups quickly on my phone, between meetings. If it's fast and obvious, great. If it fights me, I'm not going to fight back for long.

## Their report, written after the visit

### P03: Priya

#### In one paragraph
I came in to sketch Tuesday's SSC groups on my phone between meetings, and the core idea - seat people, see what buffs each group gets, move them by tapping instead of dragging - won me over fast. What slowed me down was the sheer length of the class/spec list I'd have to scroll through 25 times to actually build the roster, and a share button that gave me zero feedback when I tapped it. My session got cut off by a technical fault before I could finish checking a couple more things, but I'd already seen enough to know this tool is genuinely useful for me, just not quite frictionless yet.

#### First impression
Step 1: the front page reads as a serious WoW TBC Classic toolset - "Project Defeat," Phase 2, Alliance/Horde, cards for Character Planner, Raid Composition, Spec Tier Lists. It's clearly built for people who already know what a "spec" and a "raid buff" are, not a general audience. That's fine, that's me. I clocked "Raid Composition" immediately as the card for my task - the card description ("Plan a 10 or 25-player roster and see which raid buffs and target debuffs it actually brings") basically restated what my officer wanted from me. Right impression, right away.

#### What I came for, and what I actually did
I came to rough out groups for a 25-player SSC raid. I tapped into Raid Composition (step 2), confirmed it was already set to 25-player (step 2-3, no setup needed), and added two players through the spec picker - a Balance Druid (step 4) and a Holy Paladin (step 6). I found the actual seat grid further down (step 7-8), used the tap-based "Move" flow to move my Druid from Group 1 to Group 2 (step 9-11), renamed that seat to an actual player name, "Thaladrel" (step 12-14), checked the full buff/debuff summary at the bottom of the page (step 15), gave the raid a title, "SSC Tuesday" (step 20), and tried the "Export image" button to see how I'd share this to my raid Discord (step 18-19). I did not get through adding anything close to a full 25-player roster - two players was enough to tell me how the mechanics worked, and I didn't have the time or patience to grind through the rest on my phone in this sitting.

#### Where I got stuck or confused
- **Steps 5 and 7** (the "Add to Group" spec list): It's a long list of every class and spec with no search or filter. To add my 25 real raiders I'd have to keep scrolling past classes I don't need, every single time. It didn't reset my scroll position after each add (small mercy), but there's still no fast way to jump to "the Warrior I need" versus "the Druid I need."
- **Steps 18-19**: I tapped "Export image" expecting a share sheet, a download, or at least some confirmation. Nothing visibly happened - no toast, no preview, no new screen. I double-checked with a "look" at step 19 and there was no confirmation text anywhere on the page. I genuinely don't know if it worked.
- **Step 3**: the raid title/date/time/timezone fields sit right above the description box but don't stand out as fields at a glance - I scrolled past them the first time without registering what they were for until I came back later.

#### What I liked
- Defaulted straight into 25-player mode (step 2-3) - exactly my raid size, zero setup.
- The move flow is tap-then-tap, not drag: "press Move on their seat and then the seat you want them in" (step 9) did exactly that (step 11) - much easier one-handed than dragging a little card around on a phone screen.
- Buff counts update live. Watching Group 1's party buff count drop from 5 to 3 the moment I moved my Druid out, and Group 2 pick up 2, is exactly the kind of feedback that makes me trust the tool (step 11).
- Naming a seat opened a real text field immediately, keyboard ready, and also unfolded a clean breakdown of exactly what that player brings - Gift of the Wild, Moonkin Aura, Thorns, Innervate, Faerie Fire (steps 12-14). That's genuinely useful for buff planning, not just decoration.
- The raid title field updates the big heading above it live the moment you type (step 20) - small thing, but it's immediate confirmation the input took, which "Export image" badly needed and didn't have.

#### What I never found, or gave up on
- Never got confirmation that "Export image" actually produced anything (steps 18-19).
- Never tested the "×" remove button on a seat - I was scrolling back down to it (step 21) when the session cut out.
- Never built anything close to a full 25-player roster - only seated 2 of 25 in the time I had.

#### Words or labels I didn't understand
- "Party-scoped" (step 2) took me a second read to land, though the sentence right after it explains it fine.
- The four stat tiles - Tank / Healer / Physical DPS / Caster DPS (step 3) - showed up with no explanation of why I'd care about the split. I could guess, but it wasn't spelled out.

#### Three things I would change
1. Add a search or filter box to the class/spec picker. Scrolling a full alphabetical class list 25 times to build one raid is the single thing most likely to make me close the tab.
2. Make "Export image" (or whatever the share action ends up being) actually confirm itself - a toast, a thumbnail, a share sheet, anything. Right now it looks broken even if it isn't.
3. Give the "Move" and "×" buttons on a seat row more separation. They sit right next to each other (step 8's screenshot) - I didn't personally mis-tap, but I wouldn't want to delete a player when I meant to move them, especially rushing on a phone.

#### Would I come back? Would I recommend it, and to whom?
Yes, probably - but I'd build the initial 25-player roster on a laptop where typing and scrolling are easier, then use my phone for the fast tweaks (swap two people, rename a seat, check buff coverage) the way I used it just now. I'd recommend it to other officers in my guild who split raid-planning duty with me - the live buff/debuff view is the actual selling point, more than the seating itself.

#### Why I stopped
My session was cut off by a technical fault on the observer's side right after step 21 (a scroll), not by my own choice - the browser session closed and I was told not to start a new one. At the moment it cut out, I was scrolling back down from the "Raid title" field toward the Group 1/2 seat rows, planning to tap the "×" on a seat to confirm that removing a wrongly-added player is as easy as adding one was, before deciding whether I had patience left to keep building out the roster.

#### Plan versus reality
Plan.md said I'd scan fast, jump straight at the one thing I came for, expect big tap targets, and not fill in long forms from a DM link on a whim. That mostly held up: I went straight for "Raid Composition" off the front page with no wandering, I skimmed rather than read the explainer text in full, and I only filled in one field (raid title) rather than the date/description/timezone stuff - that's exactly the "later, at my laptop" instinct from my plan. The one thing I didn't anticipate was that I wouldn't even get through a token-sized chunk of the actual 25-person task in this sitting - between the long spec list and the interruption, I ended up doing more "does this mechanism work at all" testing (move, rename, export) than actual raid-building.
