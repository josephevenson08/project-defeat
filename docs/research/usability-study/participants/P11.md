## Their plan, written before the visit

### Plan — P11 (Dev)

#### What I personally do in my first minutes on a new site
- I skim the first screen fast to figure out what the thing actually is before I read any copy closely — if I can't tell in 5 seconds, that's already a data point.
- I look for how polished it feels: load speed, layout shift, font choices, whether animations are smooth. I notice jank immediately.
- I poke at things a normal user wouldn't: weird inputs, resizing, hitting back, reloading mid-flow, opening a second tab. I want to see if it holds together.
- I hunt for "how it's built" signals — a footer link to GitHub, an About/Changelog page, credits, tech stack mentions, anything that tells me who made this and why.
- Since a friend sent me this, I'll specifically try to get something shareable — a link, an export, a saved state — to see if I can hand it back to them in a usable form.
- I don't read documentation top-to-bottom; I click around and let the UI teach me, and only look for help text when I'm actually stuck.

#### What I'm hoping to get out of this visit
Figure out what the site actually does, judge whether it feels solid/well-engineered or held together with tape, and see if I can build something (or at least explore far enough) that I could share a link back to my friend. Bonus points if I find any "how it's built" info.

## Their report, written after the visit

### P11: Dev, 33, the curious developer

#### In one paragraph
Project Defeat is a TBC Classic (World of Warcraft) planning toolkit — character/gear/talent planner, raid composition builder, spec tier lists, raid loot tables, and professions — and it's built with more care than I expected: real WoW class colors and talent-tree prerequisite rules, live-updating stats, a genuinely useful build-sharing system, and an accurate model of TBC's party-scoped buff mechanics. It's let down by a few loose threads: one line of completely opaque copy, no confirmation feedback on two "did that even work?" actions, and a source citation that no longer matches what it links to.

#### First impression
Immediately clear: a WoW TBC Classic Phase 2 tool site (step 1), aimed at raiders who want to plan a character or a raid roster. Five cards told me exactly what was inside before I clicked anything. I was right — nothing in the rest of the visit contradicted that first read.

#### What I came for, and what I actually did
I came in hoping to judge the build quality and to get something I could actually hand back to my friend. I ran the 4-step character wizard as a Troll Enhancement Shaman (steps 3-12), poked the gear picker and talent tree to see if they were real systems or dressing (steps 16-22), found and used the Build/Share tab (step 23-24), stress-tested the "reload wipes your build" claim myself (step 26), then spent my remaining time in Raid Composition (steps 27-31) and Spec Tier Lists (steps 32-34), including following an outbound citation link.

#### Where I got stuck or confused
- **Step 14**: A line reading "The rail carries all twenty-six." sits right under the Key Totals box on the Gear tab. I expected some kind of stat explanation; I got a sentence with no discernible meaning. Confirmed it was real text, not a rendering glitch, via a second look.
- **Step 24 / step 25**: Clicking "Copy Share Link" revealed the link inline but gave no "copied!" toast or any confirmation. I expected feedback that the clipboard action succeeded; instead I was left assuming it worked.
- **Step 30**: Clicking "Export Image" on the raid roster produced no visible reaction at all — no modal, no preview, no new tab. It may have silently downloaded a file, but from the page itself I couldn't tell if it did anything.
- **Step 32**: Navigating from Raid Composition to Spec Tier Lists kept my old scroll offset instead of resetting to the top, so I landed mid-page on the Healer/Tank lists and had to scroll up to find the page's own heading.
- **Step 34**: The DPS tier list's citation link is labeled "DPS Tier List for The Burning Crusade Classic (Phase 2)," but clicking through, the actual Wowhead page is titled "...(Phase 3)". Either Wowhead moved the page forward and Project Defeat's link/label is stale, or the rankings shown no longer match their claimed source.

#### What I liked
- The wizard reskins its whole color palette to match the faction/class you pick, and buttons use real pressed/focus states (step 4, step 6, step 8) — small thing, but it's the kind of detail that tells you someone cared.
- The spec-selection button's label updates live to summarize your whole build ("Play as Troll Enhancement Shaman," steps 9-11) — nice, low-effort confirmation of what you're about to commit to.
- The gear picker (step 17) is properly modeled: full stats, sockets with a plain-English explanation of unmet socket bonuses, item level/phase/quality — and picking an item live-updated my Stamina/Intellect in the sidebar immediately.
- The talent trees (steps 19-21) are the full three-tree, ~71-talent layout with real icons and names, and it actually enforced the classic "can't skip ahead" rule when I tried to cheat into a row-6 talent with zero points spent.
- The Build tab (step 23) upfront tells you exactly what persists and what doesn't, then backs it with a real link-based export that "carries the whole build... and opens it on any device" — exactly the sharing mechanism I was hoping to find.
- Raid Composition (steps 27-31) correctly models TBC's party-scoped vs. raid-wide buffs, with live-updating coverage counts per group — genuinely deep, not just a seating chart.

#### What I never found, or gave up on
I never found an About page, changelog, credits, or any link to the site's own source/GitHub — checked the bottom of the landing page (step 2) and the full length of the Raid Composition page (step 31), nothing either place. I also didn't get to Simulation, Raids, or Professions, or the planner's Compare/Buffs/Ranked Gear sub-tabs — ran out of visit budget before I got there. I couldn't independently confirm the share link actually reached my clipboard.

#### Words or labels I didn't understand
"The rail carries all twenty-six." (step 14) — no idea what this refers to. Also a minor grammar stumble in the class-step subtitle: "Only the classes your race can actually be are offered" (step 7).

#### Three things I would change
1. Fix or remove "The rail carries all twenty-six." — right now it just looks broken.
2. Add real confirmation feedback (a toast, a checkmark, anything) to Copy Share Link and Export Image so a user isn't left guessing whether the click did something.
3. Audit outbound citations — the Wowhead Phase 2/Phase 3 mismatch (step 34) is exactly the kind of thing that erodes trust in a data-heavy tool like this once a technical visitor notices it.

#### Would I come back? Would I recommend it, and to whom?
Yes — the planner and raid comp tool are solid enough that I'd actually use them for my own casual raiding, and the share-by-link feature means I can hand a build straight to a friend, which was my whole reason for being here. I'd recommend it to my raid-lead friend specifically for the composition tool. I'd add one caveat when I share it: double-check anything data-specific against Wowhead directly, since I hit one real mismatch in ten minutes of looking.

#### Why I stopped
I'd done what I came to do — built a character, confirmed the tool isn't just a pretty mockup, found the sharing feature, and even turned up a genuine bug by following the site's own link. That felt like a natural, satisfying place to stop rather than grinding through every remaining tab.

#### Plan versus reality
Matches closely. My plan said I'd skim fast, poke at odd inputs/reload/sharing, and hunt for "how it's built" signals. I did all three. The one gap: I expected to find some footer or GitHub/about link and never did, on either page I checked bottom-to-top.
